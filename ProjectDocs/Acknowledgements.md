# Acknowledgements

The Kontinua Sequence exists because generous, talented people gave it their
time. They wrote chapters, drew illustrations, checked arithmetic, fixed typos,
found videos, built the tools that turn hundreds of LaTeX files into workbooks,
and kept those tools running. Every student who learns from these books is in
their debt, and so are we.

Thank you, all of you. What follows is only a small sample of what each person
gave the project. The commit history holds the rest.

## Aaron Hillegass

Aaron founded the Kontinua Foundation and imagined an integrated sequence of
math, physics and computer science workbooks. Aaron wrote many of the early
chapters, designed the page layout and the `ourbook.cls` document class, and has
edited and shepherded the books ever since.

## Arjan Ellingson

No one has given more to the repository than Arjan. Arjan wrote and expanded a
remarkable range of chapters: rotorcraft and quadcopters, bitmaps and number
representations, transformations, graph algorithms (including topological sort,
A\*, and many new exercises), Markov chains, APIs, circular motion, charge, and
the linear algebra chapters on subspaces and the cross product. Arjan also built
much of the machinery behind the books: the GitHub workflows that build and
deploy every chapter, the topic index and digital resources, and the reordering
of the whole sequence, along with countless fixes, such as giving every figure
consistent placement. Arjan's care touches nearly every workbook.

## Max Larish

Max wrote much of the calculus in the sequence: chapters on limits, derivative
rules, the shape of graphs, inverse trigonometric functions, methods of
integration, arc length, sequences and series, and double integrals, along with
conservation of mass and energy, kinematics, and linear combinations and span.
Max filled them with AP-style practice problems, so students can see the kinds
of questions they will meet on the exam.

## Cheery Chukwukelu

Cheery brought statistics and numerical methods to life, writing and revising
the chapters on sampling, inference for means, proportions and slopes,
chi-square tests, log transformations, statistical tables, floating-point
numbers, correlation, optimization and parametric functions. Cheery wrote the
Python files that accompany them, drew TikZ figures in the book's own colors,
added prerequisites to the chapters' digital resources, and recently added "Key
Ideas" summaries to the statistics chapters.

## Sam Baskin

Sam gave the workbooks their look, drawing a great many of the illustrations,
from atoms, eyes and balloons to helicopters, sailboats and a memorable cow, and
bringing them all into a consistent Kontinua style, with careful attention to
arrows, line weights and fonts. Sam also wrote and developed chapters, including
manufacturing, power generation, semiconductors, tides, radio, atmospheric
pressure, cognitive biases and linked lists, and documented the illustrations so
that the people who come later can build on that work.

## Pranav Gundu

Pranav made the project dependable, building the continuous integration that
builds and checks every change, adding automatic spell checking, installing the
right fonts on the build machines, and making the deploy workflow robust against
corrupted files. Pranav also cleaned up and refactored the build scripts, moved
the code listings throughout the books to `minted`, and fixed content
everywhere, from duplicated words to missing captions.

## Summer Abramson

Summer was one of the project's earliest contributors, working on the first
modules on matter and energy, discrete probability, triangles and circles,
exponents, vectors and the dot product, and cognitive biases, and drawing and
reformatting many of the early images, including the diagram of the eye.

## Mary Kadera

Mary read the books with a careful editor's eye. Mary's corrections improved
more than forty chapters, from polynomials and probability to calculus, linear
algebra and SQL, and made the text clearer for students.

## Bunny Laden

Bunny wrote and illustrated the linear algebra workbook, drafting the chapters
on vectors and matrices, linear combinations, spans and independence,
projections, Gram-Schmidt, eigenvectors and the singular value decomposition,
writing the Python files that go with them, and revising the whole workbook to
put its chapters in a better order.

## Otto Hillegass

Otto wrote the chapters on electric motors and generators and on
electromagnetic induction, and improved rocketry, orbits, chemical reactions and
the first workbook. Otto found digital resources for many chapters and fixed
build failures so that everyone else could keep working.

## Tony Cashio

Tony proofread the sequence from beginning to end, making edits across books 1
through 26, and improved the project's documentation for writers and editors.

## Allison Hopper

Allison enriched the opening chapters on matter and energy, buoyancy, units and
fertilizer with new definitions, resources, captions and a welcome sense of
humor, including potatoes.

## Devin Hadley

Devin built the tool that uploads finished workbooks and their chapter
information to the readers site, so that students can read the books in the
Kontinua apps.

## Frank Chambergo

Frank wrote the chapter on HTML, with its exercises, and started the additional
content for the chapter on HTTP.

## David Nezelek

David drafted the first versions of the chapters on limits and rational
functions, with exercises and index entries.
