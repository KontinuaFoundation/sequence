\chapter{Simple Logistic Regression}

\section{A Categorical Response}\index{logistic regression}
Linear regression predicts a quantitative response. \newterm{Logistic regression}
predicts a categorical response, most often a \emph{binary} outcome coded as 0 or 1.
The goal is to model $p = P(Y = 1)$, the probability of the positive outcome, as a function of a predictor $x$.

\section{The Model}
A straight line cannot be used for $p$, because a line can produce values below 0 or
above 1. Instead, the model uses the \newterm{log odds}, also called the logit:
$$\ln\left(\frac{p}{1 - p}\right) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x$$
The odds $\frac{p}{1-p}$ take any value from 0 to $\infty$, and their logarithm takes any
real value. Solving for $p$ gives
$$p(x) = \frac{e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 x}}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 x}}$$
This always lies between 0 and 1. The probability of the negative outcome is
$1 - p(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 x}}$.

\section{Estimating the Parameters}\index{maximum likelihood}
Linear regression chooses parameters by least squares. Logistic regression chooses
them by \newterm{maximum likelihood}: the estimates are the values that make the
observed outcomes most probable under the model. Software does this calculation for you.

\section{Interpreting the Coefficients}
Take the model $\beta_0 = -4$ and $\beta_1 = 1$. Then
$$p(x) = \frac{e^{-4 + x}}{1 + e^{-4 + x}}$$
At $x = 3$, $p = \frac{e^{-1}}{1 + e^{-1}} \approx 0.269$. At $x = 4$, $p = 0.5$. At
$x = 5$, $p \approx 0.731$. So the probability rises from 27\% to 73\% as $x$ goes from
3 to 5.

The coefficient $\beta_1$ controls how fast the odds change. Each one-unit increase in $x$
multiplies the odds by $e^{\beta_1} = e \approx 2.718$. The odds of the positive
outcome at $x = 3$ are $e^{-1} \approx 0.368$, and at $x = 4$ they are $1$.

\section{Predicting a Class}
To classify, choose a threshold, often 0.5. Predict $Y = 1$ when $p(x) \ge 0.5$. In this model,
that is when $x \ge 4$. The threshold is a choice, and changing it changes the errors the model makes.

\begin{Exercise}[label=slog1, title=Compute a probability]
Using $\beta_0 = -4$ and $\beta_1 = 1$, find $p(2)$.
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=slog1]
$p(2) = \frac{e^{-2}}{1 + e^{-2}} \approx \frac{0.1353}{1.1353} \approx 0.119$.
\end{Answer}

\begin{Exercise}[label=slog2, title=Find the threshold value]
With $\beta_0 = -4$ and $\beta_1 = 1$, for which $x$ is $p(x) = 0.5$?
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=slog2]
$p(x) = 0.5$ when $e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 x} = 1$, so $\beta_0 + \beta_1 x = 0$. Thus $x = 4$.
\end{Answer}

\begin{Exercise}[label=slog3, title=Interpret the odds ratio]
If $\beta_1 = 0.5$, by what factor does the odds of $Y = 1$ change when $x$ increases by 1?
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=slog3]
The odds are multiplied by $e^{0.5} \approx 1.649$, an increase of about 65\%.
\end{Answer}
