# Electromagnetic Induction — Notes

## Summary
Introduces Faraday's law (E = −dΦ_B/dt), magnetic flux as ∫B·dA, Lenz's law for the sign, induction in an N-turn coil (E = −NAΔB/Δt, with a worked 1000 V example), and applications (generators, transformers, induction cooktops).

## Strengths
- The worked coil example is correct (ΔΦ = 1 Wb, E = 1000 V).
- Ties the sign of the emf to Lenz's law and the rate of change.
- Short chapter with a clear list of applications.

## Weaknesses
- **Overstatement:** "if a conductor is moving through a constant magnetic field, no current will be induced." A conductor moving across field lines can have an induced emf (motional emf), and the flux through a loop changes if its area changes. The claim holds only for a fixed loop in a uniform field.
- **Thin content:** no worked example with a moving conductor, no Lenz's law example, and the flux integral is left to a later chapter.
