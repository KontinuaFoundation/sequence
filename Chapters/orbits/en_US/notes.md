# Orbits — Notes

## Summary
Introduces Newton's law of gravitation and the orbital speed needed at a given radius (≈7,544 m/s at 8.37 × 10⁶ m), then escape velocity (√(2GM/R)), geosynchronous orbit, Kepler's three laws, and a Mars orbit and escape exercise.

## Strengths
- The orbital speed (√(6.8 × 8.37 × 10⁶) ≈ 7,544 m/s) and the Mars orbit speed (≈3,557 m/s, period ≈ 6,000 s) are correct.
- The escape-velocity derivation is clear, and the √2 relationship (about 41.4% more speed) is correct.
- The geosynchronous speed (≈3,070 m/s at 42,164 km) is consistent with GM/r.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong altitude:** the geosynchronous radius 42.164 million m is about 35,800 km above the surface, not "36 km".
- **Rounding:** the Mars escape speed from the surface is ≈5,020 m/s, not 5,027.
- **Broken references:** the Mars exercise answer says `ref=circular` (should be `mars_orbit`), and the escape exercise cites `eq:escveleq`, which is not defined here.
- **Inconsistent radius:** "a radius … about 2 million meters above the ground" is then used as 8.37 million m, without saying that this is measured from the center.
- **Unsourced figure:** "maybe 6 newtons per kg" for orbital gravity conflicts with the 6.8 m/s² used earlier.
- **Open FIXME:** the escape-velocity section says it could be expanded with exercises.
