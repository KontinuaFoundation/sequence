# Electromagnetic Waves — Notes

## Summary
Separates sound (a medium-dependent compression wave) from light (an electromagnetic wave), computes wavelength from c = fλ (630 nm for a red laser, 530 nm for green), tabulates the spectrum, explains why lower frequencies penetrate walls and why microwaves are 2.45 GHz, and then covers the greenhouse effect with a Earth energy budget (23% + 7% reflected, 48% + 23% absorbed).

## Strengths
- The λ = c/f worked examples are correct (630 nm and 530 nm).
- The energy-budget paragraph is a clear equilibrium argument that a physics student can follow.
- Connects spectrum ranges to everyday objects (microwave ovens, infrared sensors, heat-seeking missiles).

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong frequencies:** Wi-Fi is about 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz (2.4 × 10⁹ and 5 × 10⁹ Hz). The text writes 10⁶ Hz.
- **Unsourced numbers:** the greenhouse-gas contribution ranges (water vapor 36–72%, CO₂ 9–26%) and the energy budget need citations.
- **Typo:** "it maybe too late" (may be).
- **Unfinished:** a FIXME says the spectrum information should come from a reference.
