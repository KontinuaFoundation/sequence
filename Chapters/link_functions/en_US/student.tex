\chapter{Link Functions}

\section{The Role of a Link}\index{link function}
A link function $g$ connects the mean of the response, $\mu = E[Y]$, to the linear predictor $\eta$, so that $g(\mu) = \eta$.
The link must map the allowed values of $\mu$ onto all real numbers, because $\eta$ can be any real number. The
inverse link $\mu = g^{-1}(\eta)$ turns a prediction on the linear scale back into the scale of the response.

\section{Common Links}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Identity}: $g(\mu) = \mu$. The response is normal, and the mean can be any real number. This is ordinary linear regression.
\item \textbf{Log}: $g(\mu) = \ln \mu$. The mean must be positive, as for counts. The inverse is $\mu = e^{\eta}$.
\item \textbf{Logit}: $g(\mu) = \ln\frac{\mu}{1 - \mu}$. The mean is a probability between 0 and 1, as for binary outcomes. The inverse is the logistic function.
\item \textbf{Probit}: $g(\mu) = \Phi^{-1}(\mu)$, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal distribution function. It is another link for binary outcomes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Working Through the Links}
For a mean of $\mu = 0.8$, the logit is $\ln\frac{0.8}{0.2} = \ln 4 \approx 1.386$. The inverse logit of 1.386
returns $\frac{e^{1.386}}{1 + e^{1.386}} = 0.8$. For a mean of $\mu = 10$, the log link is $\ln 10 \approx 2.303$, and
the inverse gives $e^{2.303} = 10$.

\section{Choosing a Link}
The link should respect the range of the response. Think of a test score, which can be any number, and a chance of passing, which must stay between 0 and 1. Use the identity link for real-valued responses, the log link for
positive or count responses, and the logit or probit link for probabilities. The choice affects how coefficients are
interpreted. Under the log link, a coefficient multiplies the mean; under the logit link, it multiplies the odds.

\begin{Exercise}[label=lf1, title=Logit value]
Find the logit of $\mu = 0.25$.
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=lf1]
$\ln\frac{0.25}{0.75} = \ln\frac{1}{3} \approx -1.099$.
\end{Answer}

\begin{Exercise}[label=lf2, title=Interpret the log link]
Under the log link, $\ln\mu = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x$. If $\beta_1 = 0.2$, by what factor does the mean change when $x$ increases by 1?
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=lf2]
The mean is multiplied by $e^{0.2} \approx 1.221$, an increase of about 22\%.
\end{Answer}
