# Inclined Planes: Summary

## What the chapter covers
Applies forces and trigonometry to objects on slopes. First, a frictionless ramp given by its angle: the moving-day trolley from Simple Machines on a 15° ramp, splitting the weight into F_g sin θ along the ramp and F_g cos θ into it, the same 300 J of work, and MA = 1/sin θ = L/V. Then it adds friction with tilted axes: the normal force and friction on an incline, a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ) for sliding, the slipping angle tan θ = μ_s, and an incline-and-pulley system.

## Key ideas
- Along the ramp: mg sin θ; into the ramp: mg cos θ, so F_N = mg cos θ.
- Pushing a load up a frictionless ramp takes F = W sin θ; the work is still W·V.
- Rotate the axes so x runs along the ramp; only gravity needs splitting.
- Sliding down: a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ); on the verge of slipping: tan θ = μ_s.
