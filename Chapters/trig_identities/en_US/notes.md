# Trigonometric Identities — Notes

## Summary
Builds the unit circle (cos and sin as coordinates), derives exact values for 30-60-90 and 45-45-90 angles, proves the difference formula for cosine from the distance formula, and derives the sum, difference, double-angle, and half-angle identities, including the squared (power-reducing) identities used in integration. It ends with a set of practice exercises.

## Strengths
- Derivations are step by step, and the cosine difference formula is proved from geometry, which is good for understanding.
- Unit-circle exercises ask students to fill in the circle, which builds memory through use.
- Clear statement of the sum/difference and double-angle boxes.

## Weaknesses
- **Algebra error:** in the tan(13π/12) example, sin(13π/12) is written as "−√6/4 = √2/4 = (√2 − √6)/4." The intermediate step is wrong; the correct sum is (√2 − √6)/4 directly.
- **Typo:** the figure label "unit_cicle" in the label name.
- **Unclear sentence:** "We can use these double-angle formulas for find half-angle formulas."
