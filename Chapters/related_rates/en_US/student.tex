\chapter{Related Rates}
\index{related rates}
Drop a pebble in a pond and a ring of ripples spreads out. The radius
grows at a steady 30 cm per second. Does the area inside the ring also
grow at a steady rate? No: the bigger the ring, the faster its area
grows. The two rates are linked by the formula $A = \pi r^2$, and calculus
tells you exactly how.

In a \newterm{related rates} problem, you know how fast one quantity is
changing and want to know how fast another one is. The secret is that
every quantity is a function of time $t$, so you can differentiate an
equation that links them with respect to $t$, using the chain rule.

\textbf{Worked example: the ripple.} When the radius is 50 cm, how fast is
the area growing?

\textbf{Solution.} Differentiate $A = \pi r^2$ with respect to $t$:
$$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt} = 2\pi (50)(30) \approx 9{,}425 \text{ cm}^2\text{/s}$$
At $r = 100$ cm, it would be twice as fast.

\section{Steps to Solve Related Rates Problems}

\index{related rates!steps}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Draw a diagram} and label the quantities that change with
variables.
\item \textbf{Write down what you know and what you want}, as rates:
$\frac{dx}{dt} = \ldots$, find $\frac{dy}{dt}$.
\item \textbf{Write an equation} that relates the quantities. (Geometry is
your friend: the Pythagorean theorem, similar triangles, volume formulas.)
\item \textbf{Differentiate both sides with respect to $t$}.\index{implicit differentiation!related rates}
\item \textbf{Substitute and solve.} Plug in the numbers only
\emph{after} differentiating. If you plug in $x = 6$ first, $x$ becomes a
constant and its rate disappears.
\item \textbf{Answer in words}, with units.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Example: The Balloon and the Cyclist}\index{related rates!example}

\textit{A balloon rises at a constant 5 m/s. A cyclist rides toward the
spot directly under it at 15 m/s. How fast is the distance between the
cyclist and the balloon changing when the balloon is 100 m up and the
cyclist is 40 m from the spot beneath it?}

Let $x$ be the cyclist's distance from the spot under the balloon, $y$
the balloon's height, and $z$ the distance from the cyclist to the
balloon. They form a right triangle, so by the \index{Pythagorean
theorem}Pythagorean theorem

\begin{equation}
z^2 = x^2 + y^2
\end{equation}

Differentiating both sides with respect to time $t$ gives

\begin{equation}
2z \frac{dz}{dt} = 2x \frac{dx}{dt} + 2y \frac{dy}{dt}
\end{equation}

We know $\frac{dx}{dt} = -15$ m/s (negative, because $x$ is shrinking),
$\frac{dy}{dt} = 5$ m/s, $x = 40$ m, and $y = 100$ m. Then $z =
\sqrt{40^2 + 100^2} = \sqrt{11600} \approx 107.7$ m, so
$$2(107.7)\frac{dz}{dt} = 2(40)(-15) + 2(100)(5) = -200$$
Therefore $\frac{dz}{dt} \approx -0.93$ m/s. The distance between them is
shrinking at about 0.93 m/s. The cyclist closes in fast, but the rising
balloon nearly cancels it out.

\section{Example: Filling a Cone}\index{related rates!cone}

\textit{A water tank is an upside-down cone 4 m tall with a radius of 2 m
at the top. Water is pumped in at 2 m$^3$/min. How fast is the water
level rising when the water is 3 m deep?}

The volume of water at depth $h$ is $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$, where $r$
is the radius of the water's surface. That is two changing variables, but
similar triangles link them: the cone is half as wide as it is tall, so
$r = \frac{h}{2}$. Then
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 h = \frac{\pi h^3}{12}$$
Differentiate with respect to $t$:
$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{\pi h^2}{4}\frac{dh}{dt}$$
Substitute $\frac{dV}{dt} = 2$ and $h = 3$:
$$2 = \frac{9\pi}{4}\frac{dh}{dt} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{8}{9\pi} \approx 0.28 \text{ m/min}$$
The level rises about 28 cm per minute. It slows down as the tank fills,
because the same volume spreads over a wider surface.

\begin{Exercise}[title={A Sliding Ladder}, label=rr_ladder]
A $10$ m ladder leans against a wall. Its base slides away from the wall at $1$ m/s. When the base is $6$ m from the wall, how fast is the top of the ladder sliding down the wall?
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=rr_ladder]
Let $x$ be the distance of the base from the wall and $y$ the height of the top. Then $x^2 + y^2 = 100$, so $2x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$. When $x = 6$, $y = 8$, and $\frac{dx}{dt} = 1$, so $\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{6(1)}{8} = -0.75$ m/s. The top slides down at $0.75$ m/s.
\end{Answer}

\begin{Exercise}[title={Your Shadow}, label=rr_shadow]
You are 1.8 m tall and walk away from a 6 m streetlight at 1.5 m/s.
\begin{enumerate}
\item How fast is your shadow getting longer?
\item How fast is the tip of your shadow moving?
\end{enumerate}
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=rr_shadow]
Let $x$ be your distance from the light and $s$ the length of your
shadow. The light, the tip of the shadow, and the top of your head form
similar triangles:
$$\frac{6}{x + s} = \frac{1.8}{s} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 6s = 1.8x + 1.8s \quad\Rightarrow\quad s = \frac{3}{7}x$$
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{3}{7}\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{3}{7}(1.5) \approx 0.64$ m/s.
\item The tip is at $x + s$, so it moves at $1.5 + 0.64 \approx 2.14$ m/s,
faster than you walk. Neither answer depends on how far you are from the
light.
\end{enumerate}
\end{Answer}

\begin{Exercise}[title={The Snowball}, label=rr_snowball]
A spherical snowball melts so that its volume shrinks at 10 cm$^3$/min. How
fast is its radius shrinking when the radius is 5 cm? (The volume of a
sphere is $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.)
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=rr_snowball]
$\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \frac{dr}{dt}$, so $-10 = 4\pi(25)\frac{dr}{dt}$
and $\frac{dr}{dt} = -\frac{10}{100\pi} \approx -0.032$ cm/min. The
radius shrinks by about 0.32 mm per minute.
\end{Answer}
