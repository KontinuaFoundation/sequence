# Diodes, Rectifiers, and Photovoltaics — Notes

## Summary
Builds from silicon's four valence electrons to n- and p-doping, then explains the diode (anode p-type, cathode n-type, a depletion region of about 0.7 V), its symbol and direction of current, half- and full-wave rectifiers, and how a solar cell reverses the diode process.

## Strengths
- Accurate doping facts (phosphorus has five valence electrons, boron three) and the 0.7 V silicon barrier.
- The current-versus-electron direction confusion is flagged explicitly.
- Diode, rectifier, and solar-cell sections build on the same p-n junction picture.

## Weaknesses
- **Misleading glass claim:** the text says the silicon bonding is "why glass is hard and stable" and why glass does not conduct. Glass is amorphous silicon dioxide, so this conflates two materials. Also "It also why" is missing "is".
- **Field versus voltage:** "How strong is this field? It is typically about 0.7 volts" mixes a potential barrier with a field strength.
- **Unclear sentence:** "2 volts in the opposite direction" should say that the battery's polarity is reversed relative to the arrow (forward bias).
- **Overstatement:** "Both n-doped and p-doped silicon are conductors" should be "semiconductors with more carriers."
- **Duplicate label:** `fig:diode-real` is used for two figures.
