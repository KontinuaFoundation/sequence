# Charge — Notes

## Summary
From the balloon-and-wall opening through quantized charge, Coulomb's law, superposition, electric fields and field lines, flux and Gauss's law, continuous distributions (λ, σ, ρ), electric potential and potential energy, and lightning. It ends with a "But..." section on the strong force and electron energy levels. Many worked answers check out (the 15.6 μN force, the zero net force on the center charge, the 252 V electron acceleration, and the 0.16 m deflection).

## Strengths
- Strong opening that makes charge concrete before any formula.
- The electron-between-plates problem is a complete, correct kinematics example.
- Field-line rules and the Gauss's-law framing are clear and carefully stated.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong footnote:** 1 kV/cm equals 10⁵ N/C, not 10⁻⁵ N/C. The next sentence (multiply by 10⁵) is correct, so the footnote contradicts it.
- **Misstatement:** "each coulomb of charge is accelerated by a potential difference of 252 volts." The 252 V is the potential difference; each coulomb gains 252 J.
- **Notation:** the solid-sphere interior potential is written as "k = ..." but it is V = (kQ/2R)(3 − r²/R²).
- **Wrong description:** "This charge acts parallel to the secant line that touches both point charges only once." The force acts along the line joining the charges.
- **Typos:** "ballon," "a net electrical charge" (should be "an"), "prevent charge flowing" (prevents), "a doing positive work."
- **Incomplete sentence:** "This was shown back in Because it is quantized..."
- **Unfinished:** several ADD/FIXME comments (effective nuclear charge, water-density explanation, lightning diagram).
