# Introduction to Sequences — Notes

## Summary
Defines sequences, recursive and explicit formulas, and arithmetic sequences (common difference, with a worked 7th-term example) and geometric sequences (common ratio, with flipping sign examples). Practice exercises cover identifying each type and writing both formulas.

## Strengths
- The 7th-term example shows why an explicit formula is worth having, before it is introduced.
- Clear recursive-versus-explicit contrast with matching worked numbers (a₇ = −21 both ways).
- Exercises ask students to first identify the type, then write formulas.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong statement:** "For positive ratios (r > 1), the sequence is increasing. For negative ratios (r < 1), the sequence is decreasing." A negative ratio makes the sequence alternate in sign; it does not make it decrease. The 0 < r < 1 case is the decreasing one.
- **Wrong answer:** {−5, 25, −125, 525} is not geometric; 525/(−125) = −4.2, while the ratio is −5. The fourth term should be 625. The answer's "geometric with r = −5" is wrong for the sequence as written.
- **Typos:** "so one" (so on), "common ration," a stray "$$\\" after the first explicit formula, and "Fibonacci sequence which we will cover in-depth later" lacks a period.
- **Cross-reference:** the Fibonacci sequence is promised here, but the Python chapter defines it with a minus sign (see that chapter's notes).
- **No exercises** on finding a missing term from a given term and the formula.
