# Arc Lengths — Notes

## Summary
Derives the arc-length formula from inscribed polygons and the Mean Value Theorem, ∫√(1+f′²) dx, extends it to vector-valued functions, applies it to the distance traveled by a spring-driven block (≈ 3.019 m versus displacement −0.0229 m), and gives practice problems including a rocket ship.

## Strengths
- The polygon-to-integral derivation is clear and correct, including the Mean Value Theorem step.
- The four arc-length setups are right (for ln x, sin x, x³/3 + 1/(4x), and ln(cos x)).
- The block distance (3.019 m) and the rocket distances (≈ 84,745 m and 84,898 m) check out.
- The length2 root (x ≈ 1.159) is correct.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong differential:** the vector-valued length formula ends with "dx" but should end with "dt."
- **Wrong coefficient:** the length2 answer gives f(x) = (2/3)(3x+4)^(3/2) + 2/9. The coefficient should be 2/9, since its derivative must be √(3x+4).
- **Incomplete example:** the artillery shell is introduced, but its flight length is never computed.
