# Sound — Notes

## Summary
Follows a firecracker explosion as a compression wave, then covers echoes and acoustics, the guitar string as a source of pitch, frequency and period, the octave and half-step (12√2 ratio, with an E-from-A exercise), overtones (2×, 3×, 4× harmonics), audible ranges, and two Python programs: a harmonics plot and a WAV file generator.

## Strengths
- The firecracker opening links chemistry, energy spreading, and sound intensity.
- The octave and half-step math is correct (1.059463¹² = 2; 440 × 2^(7/12) ≈ 659.26 Hz).
- Overtones are shown with standing-wave diagrams, and the Python programs produce audible or plottable results.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong interval:** a 4:3 frequency ratio is called "the third." It is a perfect fourth. A major third is 5:4.
- **Unclear inclusion:** "Dogs can generally hear sounds in the 65 Hz to 45 kHz range" is cited without a source.
- **Chemistry phrasing:** "the last two are gasses" (gases); "carbon($C$)" spacing.
- **Typo:** "make let's a file."
