\section{Greek Letters in Math and Science}
These are important to know, for a large variety of reasons. Let us look at the most common physics and math uses:

\begin{tabular}{c l}
Symbol & Common meaning \\
\hline
$\pi$\index{pi} & circle constant, approximately $3.14159$ \\
$\theta$\index{theta} & an angle \\
$\Delta$ & change in a quantity \\
$\Sigma$\index{summation} & sum of many terms \\
$\lambda$\index{wavelength} & wavelength \\
$\mu$ & coefficient of friction, mean, or micro- \\
$\rho$ & density \\
$\omega$\index{angular velocity} & angular velocity \\
$\Omega$\index{ohm} & ohms, a unit of electrical resistance \\
\end{tabular}

You will see a lot of equations that reference Greek letters, using them as \emph{placeholders}, \emph{constants}, or \emph{variables} for an unknown quantity. For example, the area of a circle is given by

$$A = \pi r^2$$

where $r$ is the radius of the circle. In this case, $\pi$ is a constant, and $r$ is a variable. In other cases, you might see the physics equation
$$\Delta x = v \Delta t + \frac{1}{2} a \Delta t^2$$

This is the equation for the change in position under constant acceleration, where $\Delta x$ is the change in position, $v$ is the initial velocity, $a$ is the acceleration, and $\Delta t$ is the change in time. In this case, $\Delta x$, $v$, $a$, and $\Delta t$ are all variables.

One final example that we will explore later is the equation for wavelength in terms of frequency and the speed of light:
$$\lambda = \frac{c}{f}$$
where $\lambda$ is the wavelength, $c$ is the speed of light, and $f$ is the frequency. In this case, $\lambda$ and $f$ are variables, while $c$ is a constant. We can then translate lambda into other equations as needed.

\textbf{Worked example: wavelength of a radio wave.} A radio station broadcasts at a frequency of $100$ MHz $= 1.00 \times 10^8$ Hz. The speed of light is $c = 3.00 \times 10^8$ m/s. Using $\lambda = \frac{c}{f}$,
$$\lambda = \frac{3.00 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}}{1.00 \times 10^8 \text{ Hz}} = 3.00 \text{ m}$$

\textbf{Worked example: distance under constant acceleration.} A cart starts at $v = 2$ m/s and speeds up at $a = 3$ m/s$^2$ for $\Delta t = 4$ s. Using $\Delta x = v\,\Delta t + \frac{1}{2} a\,\Delta t^2$,
$$\Delta x = (2)(4) + \tfrac{1}{2}(3)(4)^2 = 8 + 24 = 32 \text{ m}$$

\begin{Exercise}[title={Using Greek Symbols}, label=greek1]
A wave has a wavelength of $\lambda = 0.5$ m and travels at $c = 3.00 \times 10^8$ m/s. Using $\lambda = \frac{c}{f}$, find its frequency $f$ in hertz.
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=greek1]
$f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.00 \times 10^8}{0.5} = 6.00 \times 10^8$ Hz.
\end{Answer}

Generally, we will define the Greek letters when providing an equation. However, it is important to be familiar with the Greek alphabet, so that you can understand what the symbols mean when you see them in equations.
