\chapter{Deep Learning}

\section{Many Layers}\index{deep learning}
A \newterm{deep} neural network has many hidden layers. Each layer applies a weighted sum followed by a nonlinear
activation function, and each layer builds features from the layer before it. Early layers may detect simple
patterns, and later layers combine them into more abstract ones. Think of recognizing a face. First you notice edges, then eyes and noses, and then the whole face.

\section{The Chain Rule}\index{chain rule}
Training a network requires derivatives of a loss through many nested functions. The \newterm{chain rule} gives the derivative of a
composition: if $y = f(g(x))$, then
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$$
For example, let $y = \sin(x^2 + 1)$. Here $f(u) = \sin u$ and $g(x) = x^2 + 1$, so
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos(x^2 + 1) \cdot 2x$$
At $x = 1.3$, this is $2(1.3)\cos(2.69) \approx -2.339$.

\section{Backpropagation}\index{backpropagation}
\newterm{Backpropagation} applies the chain rule to every weight in the network. A forward pass computes the output and the loss. A
backward pass then starts at the loss and moves toward the input, multiplying local derivatives and reusing the values computed in
the forward pass. Each weight's gradient is the product of the derivatives along its path to the loss.

Consider a single weight $w$ with prediction $\hat{y} = wx$ and loss $L = (\hat{y} - t)^2$. The chain rule gives
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \hat{y}} \cdot \frac{\partial \hat{y}}{\partial w} = 2(\hat{y} - t) \cdot x$$
The gradient descent update from the chapter on gradient descent then moves $w$ against this gradient.

\section{Practical Points}
Deep networks are trained with minibatches of examples, using stochastic gradient descent or a variant of it. Very deep networks can
suffer from vanishing or exploding gradients, because products of many derivatives can shrink or grow rapidly. Modern activation functions
and careful initialization reduce this problem.

\begin{Exercise}[label=dl1, title=Apply the chain rule]
Find $\frac{d}{dx}(3x - 1)^4$.
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=dl1]
$4(3x-1)^3 \cdot 3 = 12(3x-1)^3$.
\end{Answer}

\begin{Exercise}[label=dl2, title=One gradient]
A single weight has $\hat{y} = wx$ and $L = (\hat{y} - t)^2$. Find $\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}$ when $w = 2$, $x = 3$, and $t = 5$.
\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}[ref=dl2]
$\hat{y} = 6$, so $\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = 2(6 - 5)(3) = 6$.
\end{Answer}
