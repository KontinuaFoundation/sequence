# Atmospheric Pressure — Notes

## Summary
Explains air pressure as force per area, derives sea-level pressure (≈101.3 kPa) from the mass of the air column, uses the barometric formula to find pressure at altitude (Everest ≈ 30.1 kPa), and applies it to a drinking straw (maximum lift ≈ 10.2 m for water), a mercury barometer (≈756 mm), a siphon, a P-trap, and a toilet.

## Strengths
- Starts with a balloon and a square meter of air, which makes pressure concrete.
- The straw calculation reduces to h = P/(ρg), which students can rederive for any liquid.
- Real-world examples (siphon, toilet, P-trap) are memorable and practical; the gasoline safety note is appropriate.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong word:** "the value is open" should be "the valve," and "the float is too low" should be "when."
- **Figures:** a FIXME says the three siphon figures need an order or caption; several figures have no explanatory text.
- **Typo:** "weights" should be "weighs" (the water in a cubic meter).
- **Inconsistent numbers:** the mercury example uses 100 kPa, while the sea-level value is 101.3 kPa; the chapter should say which is used.
- **No exercises** beyond Everest; the straw and siphon sections have no practice problems.
