# Introduction to the Kontinua Sequence — Notes

## Summary
A short motivational opener. It argues that becoming a modern problem solver is hard but worthwhile: engineers, scientists, doctors, programmers, architects, actuaries, and mathematicians are in demand and well paid. It encourages persistence ("solving problems is like a muscle"), reassures students that they need not be the best in the world to be in the top 10%, and closes with Feynman's "atoms" sentence as a starting point.

## Strengths
- Warm, direct second-person voice that speaks to high school students.
- The Feynman anecdote gives a memorable, concrete entry point into physics.
- Frames the whole book around the tools of math, computers, and science.

## Weaknesses
- Statistics (6% of jobs now, above 10% soon, "twice the salary") have no source or date; they will look dated and may be challenged.
- The "10 million people your age" figure is unsourced and vague.
- Ends abruptly on "That seems like a good place to start" with no roadmap of the book's chapters or how to use the Sequence.
- Minor typos/spacing: double space in "help you  along", "Don't" italic emphasis placement is fine but the paragraph lengths are uneven.
- Mentions "Kontinua Sequence" without saying what it is or how the chapters connect.
- Quote is mid-sentence with a hyphen-less em dash; consider consistent punctuation.
