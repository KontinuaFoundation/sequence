# Conic Sections — Notes

## Summary
Defines the four conics (circle, ellipse, hyperbola, parabola) by their geometric property and gives standard equations: (x−h)² + (y−k)² = r², x²/a² + y²/b² = 1, x²/a² − y²/b² = 1 (and its swap), and y = a(x−h)² + k (and its swap). It notes that Desmos can graph them and ends with a short remark on the vertical line test.

## Strengths
- Each definition is a clear geometric property, and the equations follow from them.
- Lists both orientations of the hyperbola and parabola.
- Points students to a graphing tool.

## Weaknesses
- **Misleading claim:** "the other three cannot be expressed as functions, only equations." Circles, ellipses, and hyperbolas can be split into function pieces (y = ±√…). The correct statement is that they fail the vertical line test as a whole.
- **Incomplete:** the parabola VLT remark applies only to the upward/downward form; the sideways form also fails.
- **Missing content:** no focus-and-directrix worked example, though the parabola definition uses them; no asymptote discussion (a comment says to add it).
- **Figures:** two FIXMEs and an empty center block; the hyperbola diagram is missing.
- **Grammar:** "Each type of conic sections," "a double-cone" definition is loose.
- **Source:** the Wikimedia figure is linked with a long URL caption.
