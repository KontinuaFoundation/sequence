# How Eyes Work — Notes

## Summary
Compares the eye to a camera (cornea, pupil and iris, retina, sclera), explains rods and cones, then covers glaucoma, cataracts, myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism. The second half covers color: rainbow wavelengths, why 580 nm and a 650 + 550 nm mix both look yellow, pigments, and CMYK versus RGB.

## Strengths
- The camera analogy and the inverted-image point are clear.
- The disease explanations (pressure, clouding, focus position) are accurate in mechanism.
- The yellow-from-two-wavelengths argument is a good demonstration of how cone receptors work.

## Weaknesses
- **Misleading cone description:** the red cones are described as seeing "up to about 700 nm", but their peak is about 560 nm. The blue cone peak is closer to 430–440 nm than 450 nm.

- **Unclear cone logic:** the text says cones are "most sensitive" to 550 and 450 nm but never gives the red peak.
