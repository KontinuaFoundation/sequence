# Inverse Trigonometric Functions — Notes

## Summary
Introduces arcsin as the inverse of sin, using graphs to show that sin is not one-to-one, so its inverse needs a restricted domain (the vertical line test fails on the unrestricted inverse). It gives a table of derivatives of inverse trig functions and three practice problems (arctan², x·arcsec(x³), arcsin(1/x)).

## Strengths
- The two-graph argument (horizontal line on sin, vertical line on arcsin) is a clear application of earlier ideas.
- The product-rule example for x·arcsec(x³) is correct.
- Each practice answer shows the rule used.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong derivative:** the answer for arcsin(1/x) has an extra 1/x factor. The correct result is −1/(x²√(1 − 1/x²)).
- **Missing absolute values:** the table gives the derivatives of arccsc x and arcsec x without |x|. The correct forms are −1/(|x|√(x²−1)) and 1/(|x|√(x²−1)).
- **Unexplained:** the inverse-sine restriction (−π/2 to π/2) is never stated, though the discussion hints at it.
