# Manufacturing — Notes

## Summary
A survey of manufacturing processes grouped by material: subtractive processes for wood and metal (milling, lathing, CNC), metal-specific rolling, stamping, sheet-metal flanging, and casting; wood bent lamination and steam bending; plastic injection molding; and additive and cutting methods (3D printing, laser cutting, water jetting).

## Strengths
- Organized by material, which makes the sections easy to scan.
- Each process is described with what it is good for (for example, stamping for car panels, casting for toys).
- Mentions cost, yield, and time as the trade-off engineers balance.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong definition:** malleability is described as "the ability to bend without breaking." Malleability is the ability to be hammered or rolled into shape; ductility is the ability to be drawn into wire.
- **Unsupported claim:** casting "results in minimal material wastage." Sprues and gates are removed, so waste is not always minimal.
- **Missing figures:** about ten "graphic" comments describe figures that are not included; one FIXME says the water-jet graphic is missing.
- **Typos and style:** "whilst," "Almost all sheet metal parts are reinforced in this manner" (an unsupported generalization).
- **No exercises:** the chapter has no questions to check understanding.
