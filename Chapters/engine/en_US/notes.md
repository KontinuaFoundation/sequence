# The Piston Engine — Notes

## Summary
Covers the four-stroke cycle, energy losses (30% work, 35% exhaust, 30% coolant, 5% friction), lubrication, failures, engine specs, and a Model T ethanol problem with mole counts and heat. Closes with octane, the choke, and fuel injection.

## Strengths
- The four-stroke description is clear and correct.
- The Model T exercise solution is mostly right: n = 0.02466 mol of mixture, n_e = 0.001613 mol of ethanol, CO₂ = 0.003226 mol, H₂O = 0.004839 mol, and heat = 2.145 kJ.
- The ethanol combustion equation is balanced (C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O).

## Weaknesses
- **Mixed fuel:** the early text says gasoline, then "ethanol and air" enters the cylinder.
- **Wrong numbers in the Model T working:** the bore is 9.525 cm, but the working writes 9.535. The cross-section area of 71.26 is in cm², not ml.
- **Unit problem:** "the cross section is 71.26 ml" should be cm².
- **Typos:** "falls through the oil into a pan" should be "falls into a pan." "choke value" should be "choke valve." "between to 8:1" and "rT" should be RT. "H₂OI" and "ethanl" are typos.
- **Lean mixture:** the text says a lean mix gives maximum pressure, which is not a reliable description. Lean mixtures burn hotter, and the main effect is on emissions and knock.
- **Unanswered question:** the exercise asks "how rich should the mix be," but the answer never states an air-fuel ratio.
