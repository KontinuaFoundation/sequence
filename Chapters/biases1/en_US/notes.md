# Cognitive Biases 1 — Notes

## Summary
Introduces cognitive biases as universal thinking quirks and covers ten of them, each with a short description and example: fundamental attribution error, self-serving bias, in-group favoritism, bandwagon effect and groupthink, curse of knowledge, false consensus, spotlight effect, Dunning-Kruger effect, confirmation bias (with the call to design experiments in advance), and survivorship bias.

## Strengths
- Each bias has a clear definition and a relatable example.
- The confirmation-bias section gives a concrete scientific-integrity rule (state outcomes before the experiment).
- Ties the topic to the engineering goal of thinking clearly.

## Weaknesses
- **Unsourced claims:** "about 50" biases, "few topics can be explained in under 100 pages," and the Dunning-Kruger framing lack citations.
- **Oversimplified:** "the less you know, the more confident you are" misstates Dunning-Kruger, which concerns low-skill people overestimating their performance.
- **Overstated example:** survivorship bias says "only the great houses" survived; this is a claim, not evidence.
- **Unsupported aside:** "racial bias isn't a cognitive bias" is asserted without explanation.
- **Figures:** most images have no captions or labels, and the bandwagon figure is commented out.
- **Vague wording:** "more often" and "skewed sample" are used without numbers.

## Index entries added
