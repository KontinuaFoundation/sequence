# Laws, Paradoxes, Thought Experiments, and Illusions — Notes

## Summary
A tour of paradoxes that motivate proof by contradiction: Barber's, Russell's (R ∈ R ⟺ R ∉ R), Simpson's (hospital data), Monty Hall, the birthday problem, Zeno's dichotomy and arrow paradoxes, Schrödinger's cat, time travel, Penrose steps, the Möbius strip, the coastline paradox, and Moravec's paradox with neural scaling laws.

## Strengths
- The Simpson's paradox table is consistent: subgroup counts sum to the totals (800/1000 and 890/1000).
- Russell's and Zeno's paradoxes connect directly to the proofs chapter.
- Includes a Python plot for Simpson's paradox.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong probability:** the Monty Hall section says the two remaining doors are "50/50," then says the switch door has 2/3. Only the 2/3 claim is correct.
- **Wrong numbers (birthday problem):** P(no shared birthday) at k = 22 is 0.5243, not 0.5073. The k = 23 values are correct.
- **Wrong formula:** arc length is ∫√(1 + f′(x)²) dx, not ∫f(x) dx.
- **Terminology:** the hidden variable in Simpson's paradox is a confounding variable, not an "independent variable."
- **Overstatement:** "the electrons are present in both atoms simultaneously" (nonpolar bonds) is an unsupported analogy.
- **Garbled sentences:** the grandfather paradox ("prevents their grandfather from having children killing their grandfather") and the Möbius strip ("a piece of paper is a surface").
