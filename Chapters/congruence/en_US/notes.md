# Congruence — Notes

## Summary
Defines congruence through rigid transformations (translation, rotation, reflection), then builds the triangle congruence tests: SSS, SAS, and angle–angle–side. It explains why SSA does not work with a counterexample (5, 7, 45°), handles the right-triangle case, and ends with CPCTC and a Ted-the-triangle-drawer exercise.

## Strengths
- Starts with a concrete "cut out and match" picture before the formal definition.
- The SSA counterexample is clear and well chosen.
- The exercise ranges over all the tests, and the answer for the "angle not between the sides" case is right.

## Weaknesses
- **Wrong naming:** "Side-Angle-Angle" is used for the two-angle-one-side test, but the earlier paragraph calls the same idea "two angles and one side"; name it AAS or ASA consistently.
- **Missing explanation:** the SSS test is stated without the a² + b² = c² argument, which an inline comment says should be added.
- **Unclear wording:** "similar triangles... if one side is equal ... they must be the same size."
- **Unfinished:** a FIXME diagram for the congruence tests.
